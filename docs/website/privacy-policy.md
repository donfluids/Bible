# Privacy policy

Bible (org.riversresearch.bible), വേദപുസ്തകം (org.riversresearch.bible.malayalam) and வேதாகமம் (org.riversresearch.bible.tamil), for Android and iPhone, and Bibles (org.riversresearch.bible.india), for Android, published by Rivers Research LLC. Effective 26 November 2026.

> The apps collect no personal data and send nothing to us or to anyone else. They do not connect to the internet. What you save in them stays on your phone, and leaves it only when you share, copy, export or print it yourself, to the place you choose.

## What the apps keep, and where

The apps store your settings, your reading place, and the bookmarks, highlights, marked text, notes and notebook pages you make. These are kept only in the app's own storage on your phone. We never receive them and have no way to see them.

## Backup

If backup is switched on in your phone's settings, your phone can include the apps' settings, bookmarks, highlights, marked text, notes and notebook pages in its backup, so they come back when you reinstall the app or move to a new phone: on Android to your Google account, on iPhone to iCloud. Your phone does this under your Google or Apple account's terms; we cannot see the backup. You can switch backup off in your phone's settings. The Bible text itself is not included in the backup.

## Copying and sharing

When you copy or share a verse or a notebook page, or share a verse as a picture card, the text or picture goes to the clipboard or to the app you choose, and only when you ask for it. A card, with any heading and message you type on it, is made on your phone, and the apps keep no copy of it. If you put one of your own photos on a card, you choose it in your phone's own photo picker: the app sees only that photo, uses it on your phone and sends it nowhere. A card design you save keeps its own copy of the photo on your phone (left out of Android's backup; on iPhone it is included in iCloud backup); removing the design deletes the copy.

## Backup files and PDFs

You can save your notebook pages, bookmarks, highlights and notes as a backup file, or a notebook page as a PDF or a picture. The file is made on your phone and goes only where you send it in your phone's share menu (for example Files, Google Drive or a chat); it is a plain file that anyone you give it to can read. To restore, you pick a backup file in your phone's own file picker: the app reads only that file, adds what it holds to the app and sends nothing anywhere. We never receive these files.

## Reading aloud

When you have a chapter, or a Hebrew or Greek word, read aloud, the apps pass that Bible text to your phone's own text-to-speech engine, which is part of your phone, not of the apps. Only the text being read is passed to it. Most voices work without the internet. Settings → Read aloud lists the phone's voices; one marked Online is produced by the engine's maker over the internet, and the text being read goes to them under their own privacy terms.

വേദപുസ്തകം and Bibles also contain their own Malayalam voice, which reads the Sathyavedapusthakam (1910) on the phone itself. It works without the internet, and nothing it reads leaves the phone.

## Links

The Sources and licences screen has links to the websites the texts come from. They open in your web browser, and those websites have their own privacy policies.

## Writing to us

Settings has a Write to us row, and after about two weeks of reading a small card in the reader invites you to write. "Not now" puts the card off for about two months; "No thanks" removes it for good. The row or the card opens a note in your own mail app, addressed to us, with a last line naming the app, its version and build number, and your phone's system version and model (for example "Android 14, Samsung SM-S911B"; on iPhone only the iOS version), so a problem can be understood. You can delete that line, and the apps send nothing themselves: the note goes only if you send it, from your mail app, under its terms. Your address and what you write are used only to answer you and to improve the apps, and are not shared.

## What the apps do not do

- No accounts or sign-in.
- No advertising.
- No analytics, tracking or crash reporting.
- No access to your contacts, location, camera or microphone. The apps open a file only when you pick it yourself (see Backup files and PDFs), and see only that file.

On Android, the apps use two kinds of permission, and ask you for neither: vibration, for the light ticks you feel when you select a verse, mark text, save a highlight or change the text size; and the two that let reading aloud go on when you leave the app ("run foreground service" and "run foreground service of the media playback type"), with its controls in your notifications. They do not reach the internet. On iPhone the apps ask for no permissions; reading aloud goes on when you leave the app, with its controls on the lock screen; the same ticks use the phone's haptics, which need none, and the photo and file pickers are the phone's own, which show only what you choose.

## Children

The apps collect no data from anyone, including children. If a child writes to us, we use the message only to answer it, and then delete it.

## Removing your data

Uninstalling an app (on iPhone, deleting it), or choosing Clear storage for it in Android's settings, removes everything it stored on your phone. To remove a copy kept in a backup, manage your backups in your phone's settings, your Google account or iCloud.

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
